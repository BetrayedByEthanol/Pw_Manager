## 1. Product scope

Vaulty is a local-first password/passkey manager with a Rust vault engine and daemon, CLI, browser extension, native-messaging transport, trusted physical FIDO2 authenticators, minimal virtual PRF provider, password generator, recovery, authenticated client pairing and optional hotkey activation. Primary assets are local and portable; no cloud account or hosted recovery is required for the foundational product.

In scope, eventually: vault lifecycle and CRUD; per-entry protection; password generation; distinct physical/virtual factor policies; safe backups, recovery and migration; explicitly authorized browser credential save/fill; trusted clients and local service; selected passkeys; optional hotkey activation. Out of initial release: automatic sync-conflict merge, hosted team/cloud sharing, hardware-only unlock, remote internet relay, universal secure keystroke injection and guarantee against full same-user/OS compromise.

Original intent is preserved: **67 historical checklist occurrences** mapped to **34 baseline normalized functional requirements**, plus **4 v0.4.5 additions** (38 tracked requirements total). Full source wording and O-01…O-67 mapping are preserved in the [historical feature reference](history/v0.3-design-reference.md#21-original-feature-list-traceability-source-baseline).

## 2. Security architecture and keys

**One portable vault file.** The VMK (Vault Master Key) encrypts and authenticates the authoritative outer control body, including current index, entry ciphertext records, policy and trust registries, recovery-slot descriptions, generations and tombstones. The public header must be small, bounded, privacy-minimized and validated before attempting expensive work. Embedded entry records remain **independently ciphertext-encrypted** using per-entry random DEKs. After a successful load, the engine may retain authenticated metadata and opaque entry ciphertext in memory for later entry reads without the VMK.

**Separation.** The VMK MUST NOT be an input to an entry's DEK/KEK or VERS unwrapping. Entry plaintext remains protected by its own random DEK regardless of outer-file decrypt. The VERS (Vault Entry Root Secret) is independently generated and supplies the first component for ordinary default-entry access; custom-protected entries use their own entry password instead. The second component is the entry-selected physical FIDO2 PRF or virtual software-provider output. Every entry has an explicit policy and scoped wrapping slots. Neither a vault-open token nor VMK recovery grants implicit access to entry DEKs.

**Operation lifetime.** CLOSED and LOCKED hold no unlocked metadata or secret material. OPEN processes only bounded public framing. OPEN → LOADED performs fresh VMK authorization, decrypts/authenticates the outer body, and then zeroizes/discards VMK. LOADED holds authenticated metadata, opaque entry ciphertext and a two-share **masked VERS representation** (not plaintext VERS). The software virtual authenticator is available while LOADED, as a non-independent protocol adapter rather than a security factor. This is the **approved v0.4 exception** superseding v0.3's blanket prohibition on VERS/virtual-provider material in LOADED. Plain VERS is reconstructed only during a bounded operation; it, PRF outputs, KEKs, DEKs and plaintext are cleared after use. Refresh both VERS XOR shares from a fresh CSPRNG mask after reconstruction and on policy-relevant transitions. An attacker reading both shares can reconstruct VERS: masking is friction/defense-in-depth, **not cryptographic protection against full process-memory compromise**.

**Reads and writes.** A READ_OPERATION MUST authorize the selected entry factors without reconstructing VMK or prompting for the master password merely to use the already LOADED vault. For default+virtual entries, masked VERS and the LOADED virtual adapter provide the normal path; for custom-password entries a fresh entry password may be required; for physical entries a **fresh registered hardware operation on each read** is mandatory. WRITE_OPERATION changes the authoritative file and MUST freshly authorize VMK, with additional entry factors where entry secrets/policy are affected, and commit by a crash-consistent atomic transaction. No secret cached across operations beyond the specifically allowed masked VERS/virtual-provider LOADED exceptions.

**R5 — mandatory session safety floor:** LOADED MUST have a configurable inactivity timeout **and** finite absolute maximum lifetime measured from successful load; activity MUST NOT extend the absolute lifetime. OS screen/session lock, suspend and hibernate MUST cancel operations and lock before additional secret use. A failed, disconnected or unverifiable lifecycle listener MUST fail closed: deny new secret operations and clear loaded metadata, VERS shares, software virtual-provider state, approvals and staged operations. Linux should handle logind session events and `PrepareForSleep(true)` (with a delay inhibitor as appropriate); Windows must handle equivalent session/power events. Process/core-dump hardening is baseline. Timeout durations are tunable; the safety events are not.

**R6 — bounded write transaction:** From LOADED create a `WRITE_TRANSACTION` with unpredictable ID, authenticated base generation/head, finite TTL and operation cap. Stage independently encrypted records with only transient scoped entry/recovery factors; **do not retain VMK during staging**. The daemon (never browser or native relay) freezes the exact change set, binds its digest to a trusted human-readable summary, and obtains owner approval. In `WRITE_OPERATION` freshly authorize VMK, recheck the authenticated on-disk head under an exclusive writer lock, and perform **one** atomic whole-file commit with new outer nonce, durable temp, rename, parent-directory fsync and verified reopen. Clear VMK, stages and transient factors on commit, failure, cancellation, expiry or lock. A 20-entry import can fit one bounded approved commit; each entry's policy still applies.

**Telemetry** such as last-used timestamps, diagnostic activity and UI counters may be stored separately as loss-tolerant non-authoritative data. They MUST NOT become access-control state or trusted replay/lockout counters without independent protection. Software passkey counter policy requires a reviewed design.

## 3. Explicit normal entry modes and assurance

| Entry policy | First component | Second component | Assurance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ENTRY_DEFAULT_VIRTUAL | VERS (reconstructed from masked LOADED shares) | LOADED software PRF adapter | Software/password-derived assurance; **not 2FA** |
| ENTRY_DEFAULT_PHYSICAL | VERS | Fresh registered physical FIDO2 PRF, per read | Hardware-gated path |
| ENTRY_PASSWORD_VIRTUAL | Fresh entry password | LOADED software PRF adapter | Entry-password software assurance; **not 2FA** |
| ENTRY_PASSWORD_PHYSICAL | Fresh entry password | Fresh registered physical FIDO2 PRF, per read | Hardware-gated path |

**S1 — entry policy chooses provider:** presence of a hardware token in the vault does not cause virtual entries to use hardware. Physical entries cannot silently fall back to virtual; every normal read requires fresh physical operation. Scope, credential identity, RP, UV/UP requirements and PRF result must be verified before unwrapping. Do not call unverified manufacturer attestation “certified hardware.”

**S2 — ISOLATED:** an ISOLATED entry MUST use a physical normal-access policy and MUST perform a fresh registered physical FIDO2 operation for every normal read. **Every complete entry-DEK access route, including ancestor/alternate routes, MUST require cryptographic physical hardware regardless of the selected recovery policy.** Recovery is a separate choice: an ISOLATED entry MAY use `STRICT_NONE` (no entry recovery route), or MAY have recovery routes only if **each** complete recovery route also requires physical hardware. `STRICT_NONE` NEVER authorizes a software-only normal access path. In particular **ISOLATED code-only entry recovery is forbidden**. A vault-level code-only VMK recovery is separate and does not itself bypass isolated entry-DEK protection.

**Effective assurance:** Compute and display **two distinct classes** of complete normal/recovery routes: **VMK/outer-metadata** routes (revealing titles, usernames, URLs, trusted registries and other VMK body metadata) and **entry-DEK/secret** routes (with all wrapping-slot ancestors and alternatives). Display weakest configured and currently usable routes; LOADED and a virtual PRF are not independent possession factors. Hardware-vault code+existing-password administrative recovery can expose **all outer metadata without the YubiKey** without satisfying an Isolated entry's DEK path. Revoked, suspended or disabled factors never authorize access. The Python/Rust route checker is reference-only, not runtime enforcement.

**Standard hardware-tier blast radius:** One captured reusable PRF output **plus VERS** may expose all default Standard entries using that hardware-derived root, despite the mandated fresh hardware operation on each legitimate physical-entry read. Custom passwords and separately scoped roots can provide additional barriers. Shared-root batching remains provisional pending OPEN-15 review. `STRICT_NONE` disables recovery, never normal hardware requirements.

## 4. Recovery and slot provisioning

Recovery is a **locally authorized repair/rewrap procedure**, not ordinary or stealth unlock. Generate a uniformly random 256-bit recovery code with transcription checksum, one-time trusted display and confirmation. Dedicated entry/group codes and intentionally unrecoverable entries remain representable.

**R1 — owner-approved recovery routes, by effective normal VMK unlock mode:**

| Vault unlock mode | Permitted administrative recovery |
| --- | --- |
| Password-only / virtual | Recovery code **alone** restores VMK; resetting master password and restoring VERS/provider/entry access additionally needs the applicable separately provisioned wrapping slots. |
| Hardware-backed; original token unavailable, password retained | Recovery code **plus existing master password** authorizes enrolling a replacement key through a pre-provisioned administrative VMK slot. It does NOT recreate the lost token's PRF or automatically recover DEKs. |
| Hardware-backed; master password forgotten, registered token retained | Recovery code **plus already registered physical hardware** authorizes setting a new master password via a distinct pre-provisioned administrative VMK slot. |
| Hardware-backed; master password and all registered hardware unavailable | No authorized recovery. |

Hardware-backed vaults MUST provision **both** separate administrative two-factor routes and MUST NOT include code-only VMK/metadata recovery, opt-in or emergency. A vault with any normal software-only VMK route must be labeled by its weakest effective unlock mode rather than called hardware-backed. Standard entry recovery in hardware vaults may use code+password or code+registered hardware only if independently sealed and policy-valid; Isolated recovery must always include physical hardware or be STRICT_NONE. Neither VMK recovery route automatically unwraps VERS, provider bootstrap or entry DEKs.

Recovery routes are **not implicit**. An outer VMK recovery slot, a VERS recovery slot and an individual entry-DEK recovery slot protect different keys. A recovered VMK never automatically recovers VERS or independent DEKs. Available approved policies include shared/inherited, group-schema, INDEPENDENT and STRICT_NONE; support for user-facing INDEPENDENT setup is deferred (S3), while its schema/checker remains intact. Code-only administrative recovery is permitted **only for password-only vaults**. Hardware-backed vaults MUST NOT create code-only VMK/metadata recovery slots, including emergency or opt-in alternatives. Standard entry-DEK recovery in hardware vaults must use actual separately provisioned code+existing-password or code+registered-hardware routes; Isolated DEK routes require hardware or STRICT_NONE. Recovery is never a virtual substitute for FIDO2.

**Option A — require code when provisioning a code-dependent slot (accepted 6 November 2026):**
- Normal read/edit through an already valid route does **not** demand the recovery code.
- Creating **new or replacement code-dependent** recovery envelopes requires the applicable actual code and every other configured slot factor. Entries inheriting an already verified shared recovery root need no new code prompt when they seal no new code-dependent slot.
- Adding a new hardware authenticator requires the code **and** that new device's verified PRF for affected recovery slots; never mark a token fully protected before verification.
- Recoverable ISOLATED entries require the relevant code **and authorized physical hardware on every complete entry recovery route**; ISOLATED normal reads always require fresh physical hardware, even under `STRICT_NONE`. `STRICT_NONE` disables entry recovery slots rather than weakening normal entry authentication.
- Master-password changes must request the code when rebuilding code+password slots.
- VMK or recovery-code rotation requires the old relevant code and all other necessary factors to rebuild dependent slots.
- Missing prerequisites must fail closed with no misleading “protected” success; preserve prior valid slots until replacement is verified.
- Status is explicit: READY, PENDING, MISSING, DEGRADED, UNVERIFIED or STRICT_NONE. No silent protection downgrade.

Do not implement an unreviewed public-key escrow shortcut to evade this explicit-code policy. Recovery-code presentation and storage are sensitive operations; never write them to logs, browser local storage or normal telemetry.

### OPEN-02 — virtual-provider seed and pre-load bootstrap

Generate the virtual-provider seed randomly and independently of the master password. Seal the **same seed** under separate password-derived normal and authorized recovery-derived bootstrap slots. A password-only vault requires a code-derived seed recovery slot to restore the provider after password loss. Hardware-backed bootstrap routes must satisfy the distinct R1 code+existing-master and code+already-registered-hardware administrative factors. If the virtual provider is needed for the initial VMK unwrap, its protected seed bootstrap MUST be reachable **before** decrypting the outer body; it cannot exist only inside that encrypted body. Password changes rewrap the original seed instead of regenerating it. Bootstrap encryption/commitment remains part of OPEN-01 security review.

### Hardware PRF and user-verification enrollment invariant

Record and cryptographically authenticate the required UV mode, credential/RP identity and exact PRF salt/scope per hardware wrapping slot; bind these settings to slot AEAD AAD. During enrollment, obtain and verify **two stable PRF derivations** with the same credential/salt/UV mode before sealing. A UV/PIN mismatch or unsupported hardware operation MUST fail closed, never silently retry a weaker non-UV or virtual provider. Exact hardware support and salt/UV semantics remain subject to OPEN-15 review.

## 5. Vault history, rollback and migration

**S4 — authenticated ancestry and deterministic conflict rule:** Every committed body binds a stable vault UUID, unique commit ID, generation, hash of its predecessor's **encrypted serialized file**, stable entry IDs/revisions and deletion tombstones. Preserve enough authenticated ancestry to recognize known predecessors versus divergence. Under exclusive writer lock compare the current head against the transaction's authenticated base. On stale base, unknown required ancestry or divergent authenticated heads, **refuse the write**, preserve **both encrypted versions** (one as a clearly marked encrypted conflict copy), and require explicit owner reconciliation. Never silently select a winner or open the conflict copy as the active file.

**Local accidental-rollback detection:** Store owner-private last-seen vault UUID/head hash/commit ID **outside the vault directory**. Compare after VMK authentication/decryption but **before granting LOADED**. A restored older copy, unexpected missing/reset local record or ambiguous divergence requires explicit first-use/recovery reconciliation. A local file is not a trusted anti-rollback anchor against same-user attackers; do not claim malicious rollback resistance.

**R8 — backup factor inventory:** Before every migration, recovery, rewrap, key rotation and password-only→hardware conversion, take a durable verified **encrypted snapshot**. Inventory known copies with stable IDs, authenticated hash/generation and **the factor combinations that can open each historical copy**, without storing factors themselves. Offer purge-on-rotate and warn that previously synced, offline or stolen backups may remain decryptable with old factors. Conversion must explicitly flag older code-only snapshots. A single `.previous` file is not a full versioned inventory.

Old VLT1 and JSON prototypes are NOT the canonical format. Format migration must identify version, validate and authorize source decrypt, take a durable backup, transform into canonical structures, write a separate destination atomically, reopen and verify, and never silently re-interpret an unknown or malformed file. Untrusted source deserialization must be bounded and non-panicking.

**N-01 — format migration (P0, FR-MIG-01):** safe one-way source-version import/upgrade with explicit rollback and compatibility report.  
**N-02 — vault-to-vault copy/move (P1, FR-XFER-01):** move or copy selected entries with source authorization and fresh destination policy/key/nonce generation; delete source only after verified durable destination commit.  
**N-03 — distinct-vault merge (P2, FR-MERGE-01):** explicit user-mediated import into a chosen destination, preserving source until verified; compare identities, versions, IDs, conflicting content, policies, recovery requirements, tombstones and ancestry. Never transplant source DEKs or trust relationships as if they were destination-authorized.  
**N-04 — automatic sync-conflict merge (P3, FR-SYNC-01):** **deferred**, not promised by N-03. Needs a separately reviewed distributed ancestry/conflict/rollback design.

N-01–N-03 share a transaction/import engine but do not imply automatic conflict resolution. Data movement is not permission to silently weaken an entry's effective assurance.

## 6. Client, daemon and UI trust boundary

The **daemon** owns policy validation, key operations, vault state, password generation, duplicate checks, persistence receipts and trusted authenticator/client registries. CLI, hotkeys, extension and future passkeys must use the same authorization engine.

A browser page/content script is untrusted. Extension UI and background perform origin/frame/gesture scoping, but the daemon independently validates everything. The native-messaging host must be a **thin opaque relay**, unable to inspect decrypted credentials, and MUST NOT independently claim a real save or unlock. A paired client identity is bound to a specific installation/instance, stored in the vault's authenticated trusted-client registry with capabilities and revocation. Removing a client requires fresh owner approval to re-pair; “same browser” is not implicit trust.

Extension ↔ daemon sensitive operations require an independently reviewed **authenticated end-to-end encrypted protocol**, explicitly approved local pairing, fingerprint verification, replay prevention and per-operation permission checks. Socket file permissions or same-user credentials alone are not an authorization proof. Fill must be initiated by a user gesture, bound to exact origin/tab/frame/entry/action and resistant to page-driven automatic disclosure. Passkey signing additionally binds RP ID and challenge.

## 7. Fail-closed safety and implementation separation

The currently merged binary deliberately exits unsuccessfully. Legacy CLI and crypto modules remain compiled but are **not** a production-ready implementation of this specification. Native host and daemon currently contain mock responses; reference-policy tests do not grant access to real credentials.

**Never enable real credential storage** until a reviewed canonical format, combined-factor KDF and key-commitment design, runtime authorization, trusted prompt/daemon, tests, recovery, rollback disclosure, and independent security review pass release gates.

Do not claim any of these from green Rust compilation, mock receipt tests, Python/Rust probe parity, or from this document alone.

**Target negative tests:** Reject hardware-vault code-only VMK routes; verify both R1 hardware recovery alternatives and metadata exposure; recover the same virtual-provider seed after password-only reset; reject Isolated DEK bypasses and UV/AAD mismatch; stage records with no VMK then approve one frozen daemon-generated transaction; cancel on lock/TTL/monitor failure; reject stale heads while preserving encrypted conflict copies; detect old-file restore and reset local state; verify pre-operation backups, factor inventory and purge warnings. These are **required implementation tests**, not evidence that they already pass.

## 8. Unresolved security decisions (block release)

- OPEN-01: factor-combiner, HKDF transcript and multi-key/key-substitution/commitment review — candidate vectors only; independent review pending.
- OPEN-15: precise physical FIDO2 PRF salt, domain/slot/entry scope, batching, freshness, attestation/UV and device capabilities — pending independent review.
- Authenticated full-file/slot envelope canonical encoding, rollback anchors, migration downgrade handling, E2E pairing protocol and trusted UI/input compromise boundary must be reviewed before corresponding feature gates.
- Permitted code-only recovery for non-isolated targets, hardware-mandatory ISOLATED normal access plus hardware-only-or-`STRICT_NONE` entry recovery, and Option A provisioning are approved **policies**, not a claim that the implementation is safe today.
